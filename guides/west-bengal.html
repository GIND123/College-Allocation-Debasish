Who can apply

Everyone needs a qualifying NEET-PG 2026 percentile, internship completed by 30 September 2026, and registration with the West Bengal Medical Council or NMC before admission (registration with another state council must be moved to WBMC).

  • Other-service candidates (3 years' regular service in West Bengal under the state or central government, not the health department) need the employer's permission. If they take a stipend, they are treated as open candidates and serve the bond.
  • Open candidates still doing another course, or its bond, are not eligible unless it ends by 30 September 2025. In-service candidates who already hold or are doing a PG degree cannot take part.
  • Foreign medical graduates need permanent registration and proof of internship in West Bengal for state quota seats.

How seats are shared

  • 40% of state quota degree seats and 50% of state quota diploma seats are reserved for in-service teachers and medical officers of the health department (WBMES, WBHS, WBPHAS). Open seats left vacant can go to in-service candidates and the other way round, on merit.
  • 5% of state quota seats go to benchmark PwD candidates. PwD status is verified by the primary medical assessment board at NRS Medical College, Kolkata, or a board listed by MCC, and only for those who applied for NEET-PG as PwD.
  • SC, ST and OBC seats follow the Backward Classes Welfare Department's gazette of 18 May 2026. Only caste certificates issued in West Bengal count; reserved-category candidates from other states compete as unreserved for state quota seats (they may still get reserved seats in the central institutes in the state).
  • OBC candidates need a non-creamy-layer certificate issued on or after 1 April 2026; creamy-layer OBC candidates apply as unreserved. EWS needs a certificate in the WBMCC format issued on or after 1 April 2026 and valid for 2026–27. There are no EWS seats for in-service candidates.

Conversion of vacant seats in Round 3

  • Open ↔ in-service; UR-PwD → UR, EWS-PwD → EWS, SC-PwD → SC, ST-PwD → ST, OBC-PwD → OBC; SC ↔ ST; EWS → UR (open seats only).
  • SC, ST or OBC seats do not become unreserved without the Backward Classes Welfare Department's permission.
  • Vacant NRI seats can become management quota seats in the online stray round, with WBMCC's permission.

Payment failed?

Check your bank account before paying again, and wait at least an hour after a failed payment. The registration fee is never refunded, and a duplicate payment can only be claimed back after reconciliation.

Management and NRI fees are set by each private college and shown on the counselling website; WBMCC does not handle fee disputes, so ask the college about the fee and the mode of payment before you fill those choices.

 There is no discontinuation bond for DNB courses at present. Course length: 3 years for MD/MS/DNB degrees, 2 years for diplomas.
  • Open candidates sign a notarised discontinuation and indemnity bond on non-judicial stamp paper of at least ₹100 at admission.
  • In-service candidates sign a discontinuation bond for the college and a post-course service bond under the TR rules for the health department.
  • A seat can be surrendered with the bond penalty only after every round of the 2026–27 counselling is over.

Step by step

  1. Register and apply at wbmcc.nic.in with your NEET-PG roll number, application number, name, mother's name and date of birth. Details can be unlocked and corrected until you pay, not after. You are asked afresh about OBC status and whether your caste certificate is from West Bengal.
  2. Pay the ₹3,000 fee, and the deposit if you want private college choices, then download the candidate profile letter. It names your verification venue, date and time slot.
  3. Physical document verification before choice filling, at the venue in your profile letter (Swasthya Bhaban, Salt Lake, for in-service candidates), with originals and one set of self-attested copies. Collect the server-generated verification certificate and check it before signing. Missing your slot means paying again for the next round. NRI candidates skip this step.
  4. Fill, save and lock choices once you are on the verified list. Saved choices are locked automatically when the window closes; unsaved ones are lost. Open and in-service candidates see only their own seats (both see management seats); management-only candidates see only management seats.
  5. Allotment by NEET-PG rank, category, PwD status and candidature.
  6. Report to the allotted college for a second, final document check and pay the college fee. If your caste or PwD papers fail there, a reserved seat is cancelled for that round and you continue as unreserved or non-PwD if still eligible. Give your willingness for the next round at admission, or you cannot take part in it.

How the rounds work

Upgrading is final

If you take part in the next round and are upgraded, you have no claim on your earlier seat. Fill only choices you would actually take.

Round 1

  • Free exit: not reporting costs nothing
  • Surrender allowed without penalty
  • Give willingness at admission to move on to Round 2

Round 2

  • New registrations allowed
  • Not reporting forfeits the deposit
  • Surrender allowed, but the admission fee is lost

Round 3

  • Vacant-seat conversions apply
  • No surrender, no upgrade
  • Not reporting ends your counselling

Online stray round

  • Fresh registration allowed
  • No upgrade
  • Closed to anyone admitted in Round 3
  • Candidates not allotted, or allotted but not reporting in Round 1, can fill fresh choices in Round 2 without paying again. Not reporting in Round 2 means paying the fee and deposit again for Round 3.
  • Anyone who surrenders a seat after admission, or is cancelled for missing essential documents, is out of the counselling.
  • A surrender counts only with the server-generated surrender letter from the college; otherwise you still show as admitted.
