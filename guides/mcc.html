1. Overview

The MCC (under DGHS, Ministry of Health) runs online counselling for these PG seats:

  • 50% All India Quota (AIQ) seats of government medical colleges
  • 100% seats of Deemed Universities
  • Central Universities / Institutes: DU, AMU, BHU, IP University central institutes, CIP Ranchi, LGBRIMH Tezpur
  • 100% DNB seats (not in-service DNB)
  • AFMS institutes (Priority III, IV and V candidates)

There are 4 rounds: Round 1, Round 2, Round 3 and the Stray Vacancy Round. Allotment depends on NEET-PG rank + your choices + your eligibility. State quota and private college seats are handled by your State counselling authority, not MCC.

    Deadlines that catch students out

    • Registration closes at 12 noon, but payment stays open until 3 PM the same day. Registering without paying in full doesn't count.
    • Choice locking opens at 4 PM the day registration closes and ends at 10 AM the next morning (Rounds 1–3). It is only an 18-hour window.
    • In the Stray round, everything ends the same day: choice filling and locking both close at 11:55 PM on 21 Dec.
    • Reporting windows are only 8 days. Float candidates should upload documents within the first 2–3 days.
    • Round 3 has no resignation, so decide carefully during the Round 3 choice filling (27 Nov–2 Dec).
    Date clash to note: the session officially starts on 20 Nov, while Round 2 reporting runs until 23 Nov and Rounds 3 and Stray continue into December. Candidates joining in later rounds will start after the session has begun.

    2. What's new in 2026: Freeze vs Float

    Until 2025, every candidate had to travel to the allotted college with original documents, even if they only wanted to upgrade in the next round. From 2026, after a seat is allotted you choose one of two paths:

    ❄️ FREEZE: "I'm happy with this seat"

    • No upgradation in the next round
    • Upload documents on the MCC portal (one-time)
    • Go physically to the college with originals
    • The college verifies documents and you pay the admission fee
    • Documents valid → admission confirmed. Discrepancy → admission cancelled

    🌊 FLOAT: "I want a better seat next round"

    • Give willingness for upgradation online
    • Upload documents on the MCC portal (one-time)
    • No need to travel. The college's nodal officer verifies online
    • If there is a query (blurred copy, wrong certificate, category mismatch, missing document), re-upload quickly
    • Verified → provisional seat confirmed and you can fill choices for the next round
    Upload documents within 2–3 days of the final allotment. This leaves time to fix any query raised by the college. If you miss the deadline, the seat can be cancelled.
    After the Round 3 result, reporting in person is mandatory for everyone allotted in Round 3, and for Float candidates of Round 1/2 who did not get upgraded.

    Resigning (surrendering) a seat

    • Freeze candidates: must go to the college in person to resign within the scheduled time.
    • Float candidates: resign online via Candidate Login → "Resignation/Surrender".
    • Resignation is allowed only for Round 1 and Round 2 seats. Round 3 seats cannot be resigned.
    • Once you resign, you have no claim on that seat.
    • All admissions and resignations must be generated online through the MCC portal. Offline letters from colleges are not accepted.
    Note: The bulletin (Annexure-6) says the new upgradation scheme still needs approval from the Supreme Court. Watch for updates.

    3. Step-by-step process

    1. Registration & fee payment: online only on mcc.nic.in. Use the same mobile number and email you gave NBE. These are pre-filled, and OTPs go only there. You can submit only one registration form; duplicates mean you are debarred.
    2. Choice filling & locking: select courses and colleges in order of preference, then lock them.
    3. Seat allotment: a provisional result comes first, then the final result. Don't book travel on the provisional result.
    4. Download the allotment letter and decide: Freeze or Float.
    5. Upload documents (mandatory for both, one-time for all rounds).
    6. Join physically (Freeze) or get online verification (Float).
    7. No seat? You move to the next round automatically (as per rules) without registering again.
    Use a laptop or computer with a recent browser and stable internet for registration and choice filling. Candidates with international numbers must also give an Indian mobile number.

    4. Round-by-round rules

    Stray VacancyRemaining seatsForfeiture Must joinNo

    Virtual vacancy: a Round 1 seat whose holder chose to upgrade. You can choose it in Round 2, but you get it only if the holder actually moves up.

    Each round is separate and has its own rules. Once a round starts, its rules apply to everyone, including people allotted in earlier rounds.

    Who can take part in Round 2?
    • Registered in Round 1 but got no seat
    • Round 1 seat cancelled at verification because of a category change (e.g., reserved → UR, PwD → non-PwD), but still eligible under the new category
    • Joined the Round 1 seat and chose "Yes" to upgrade
    • Took the free exit in Round 1 (log in with old details)
    • Did not register in Round 1: fresh registration allowed

    If upgraded in Round 2, the Round 1 seat is automatically cancelled. You must take online relieving and join the new seat, or you lose the deposit. If you change your mind about upgrading, simply don't fill fresh choices for Round 2 and you keep your Round 1 seat.

    Who can take part in Round 3?
    • First-time registrants (fresh registration fee)
    • Registered but not allotted in Round 1 and Round 2
    • Exited Round 2 with forfeiture, or resigned from Round 2: can join with fresh payment of fees
    • Holding a Round 1/2 seat with "Yes" to upgrade

    Not eligible: those holding a Round 1/2 AIQ seat who did not choose to upgrade.

    Fresh choices must be filled. Unused reserved seats are converted (see the table below).

    Who can take part in the Stray Vacancy Round?
    • Candidates not holding any seat (AIQ, State or Deemed), including fresh registrants
    • Fresh registration and fresh choice filling are required. Round 3 choices are not carried over.
    • Not eligible: those allotted in Round 3 who did not join
    • MCC and the States share data after State Round 3. Anyone on the State's list is removed before the stray round.

    Seat conversion (happens only in Round 3)

    Applying for both AIQ and Deemed? You pay only the higher amount (₹5,000 + ₹2 lakh).

    When is the deposit forfeited (lost)?

    • You are allotted a seat in Round 2, Round 3 or the Stray round and do not join
    • You resign or surrender a Round 2 (or later) seat
    • Your admission is cancelled for any reason, e.g., wrong information at registration or failure to produce documents
    • You give incorrect eligibility information for an internal quota
    Round 1 non-joining is a free exit, so the deposit is safe.

    Fees and bonds of individual colleges (especially Deemed Universities) are not MCC's responsibility. Confirm them with the college before filling choices.

    8. Deemed Universities & NRI quota

    • MCC counsels 100% of Deemed seats in all 4 rounds, including the stray round (Supreme Court order of 12.12.2022).
    • 15% of Deemed seats are reserved for NRI candidates.
    • Some Deemed Universities have Jain or Muslim minority seats. Check your eligibility.
    • An NRI-quota candidate cannot also claim minority or any other quota. NRI candidates can still compete for Deemed merit seats.
    • OCI cardholders are treated on par with Indian citizens and are eligible for UR and NRI seats.

    Who counts as an NRI sponsor?

    A parent, real brother or real sister who is an NRI living abroad. A close blood relative (uncle, aunt, grandparent, first cousin) also qualifies if they are an NRI living abroad and have actually looked after the candidate as a guardian, with an affidavit and proof.

    NRI sub-categories

    Category I: NRI candidate / parents / real brother or sister

    Birth certificate · Class 10 & 12 marksheets (or AIU equivalence) · MBBS 1st–3rd prof. marksheets · FMGE scorecard (if MBBS abroad) · NEET-PG scorecard · Candidate's passport · NRI certificate from the Embassy or Consulate · Notarised ₹100 affidavit from the sponsor promising full fees and living costs, backed by an NRE/NRO passbook · Relationship verified by a revenue officer (Tehsildar/Patwari).

    Category II: OCI candidates

    Birth certificate · Class 10 & 12 · MBBS marksheets · FMGE (if abroad) · NEET-PG scorecard · Passport · OCI card.

    Category III: Foreign nationals

    Birth certificate · Class 10 & 12 · MBBS marksheets · FMGE (if abroad) · NEET-PG scorecard · Foreign passport · Citizenship certificate.

    Category IV: Indian → NRI (changing category for counselling)

    Same academic documents + passport + the NRI's certificate + proof of relationship. A notarised affidavit from the guardian must state that they have genuinely cared for and financially supported the candidate, and will fund the full course through an NRE account. Supporting proof: fee receipts paid by the guardian, education records, bank or payment records.

    New: since candidates are adults, a court guardianship order (Guardians and Wards Act) is no longer needed. The affidavit alone is not enough without genuine supporting proof.

    NRI verification process

    1. Select NRI quota at MCC registration (even if you chose NRI in the NBE form)
    2. Pick your sub-category (I–IV)
    3. Upload documents
    4. MCC's NRI committee checks them online
    5. If there are problems, you get an SMS/email and a correction window
    6. Approved → category converted to NRI (provisionally) and NRI seats become visible
    7. Missed Round 1? You can still convert and take part from Round 2

    Bring all originals at admission. Conversion online is provisional.

    9. Persons with Benchmark Disabilities (PwBD)

    New NMC Guidelines 2026 (letter dated 16.09.2026). The main idea: eligibility depends on whether you can do the work of the chosen specialty (with reasonable support), not only on your disability percentage.

    Basic requirements

    • Benchmark disability of at least 40% (any of the 21 disabilities under the RPwD Act 2016)
    • A valid UDID card
    • A condition that is stable, without frequent flare-ups
    • An Eligibility Certificate (earlier called "Disability Certificate") from a designated MCC Medical Assessment Board, generated online before choice filling. Certificates from other hospitals are not accepted.

    What to carry to the Assessment Board

    • NEET-PG 2026 scorecard
    • UDID card
    • Self-certified affidavit of functional abilities (Appendix A)
    • Disability-specific affidavit: B (hearing), C (upper limb), D (lower limb), E (mental illness/SLD/ASD/speech), F (visual)
    • Appendix G (declaration): mandatory for all PwBD applicants

    Other disabilities (blood disorders, chronic neurological conditions, etc.) submit Appendix A only, plus G.

    Possible outcomes

    • Eligible
    • Eligible with reasonable accommodation The certificate lists the support needed.
    • Ineligible The board must give a written, reasoned order explaining which skill is affected and why support won't help.

    Nobody can be rejected only because of diagnosis, category or percentage. The certificate is valid for this session only. A fake UDID or affidavit means seat cancellation and legal action.

    Suitability by specialty group (simplified)

    Group A (surgical skills): Anaesthesia, Surgery, OBG, Ophthalmology, Orthopaedics, ENT, Emergency Medicine, PMR, Sports Medicine, etc.
    Group B (other clinical): Medicine, Paediatrics, Psychiatry, Dermatology, Radiology, Radiotherapy, Respiratory Medicine, Community Medicine, etc.
    Group C (pre-clinical): Anatomy, Physiology, Biochemistry, Biophysics.
    Group D (para-clinical): Pharmacology, Pathology, Microbiology, Forensic Medicine.

    10. Armed Forces Medical Services (AFMS)

    AFMS PG seats are mainly for serving officers. Surplus seats go to others in this priority order:

    PriorityWhoCounselled by
    I A / I BPermanent / Short Service Commissioned AFMS officersDGAFMS (during MCC Round 1)
    IIFriendly foreign country nominees; AFMS officers on study leave
    IIIDoctors sponsored by paramilitary or other GoI organisationsMCC (Round 2)
    IVEx-SSC AFMS officers within 3 years of release
    VCivilian candidates

    Key conditions for civilians (Priority V)

    • Age under 30 on 31 Dec 2026 (born on or after 01 Jan 1997)
    • Must pass a military medical fitness exam before admission
    • Must serve 5 years as a Short Service Commissioned officer after PG
    • Bond: ₹60.5 lakh (MD/MS/DNB) or ₹55.5 lakh (MDS) if you refuse the commission
    • Leaving mid-course: about ₹20.17 lakh per year of study. Early release from service: ₹12.1 lakh per unserved year.
    • Must finish PG before age 35, otherwise you become "Non-Service Liability" and the bond applies
    • Wasting an AFMS seat (cancelling, not reporting, leaving mid-course) means a 3-year debarment from AFMS counselling

    Full rules: AFMS bulletin on afmcdg1d.gov.in. Disputes go only to courts in New Delhi.

    11. Documents checklist for joining

    Bring originals. The college keeps them until you upgrade or resign.

    • Certificates must be in English or Hindi. Translations must be certified by a Gazetted Officer.
    • OBC and EWS income must be based on the financial year ending 31 March 2026.
    • A letter saying "originals are deposited elsewhere" is not accepted.
    • Name spelling differs across documents? Carry an affidavit proving they belong to you.
    • Colleges may ask for extra documents, so check with the college before going.

    12. Choice filling tips

    • There is no limit on choices. MCC suggests about 30–40.
    • Order matters: you get the highest choice available at your rank. Never list a seat you wouldn't join.
    • Make a draft list on paper first, after studying the seat matrix.
    • Check each college's fee, stipend, bond and service rules on its website before choosing. MCC takes no responsibility for these.
    • Check previous years' allotments on mcc.nic.in → Archives → PG to estimate your chances (indicative only).
    • Fresh choices are needed in every round.
    • Unlocked choices are auto-locked at the deadline. Locked choices cannot be changed, even by MCC. Lock early and print your list.
    • Keep your password private. MCC cannot retrieve it; reset it using your security question.

    13. Refunds

    • Refunds happen only after all rounds are over. Don't email before that.
    • The money goes back to the same account or card that paid. Keep it active.
    • MCC publishes the eligible list. The Financial Custodian (HLL Lifecare Ltd) starts refunds within 15 days and completes them within 30 days.
    • No interest is paid.
    • Don't raise a chargeback with your bank. It blocks the direct refund and causes long delays.
    • Don't pay from an NRI (NRE) account. Transfer to an NRO account first.
    • Paid twice by mistake? Contact the custodian 10 days after registration closes. They deduct 50% of the registration fee or ₹500, whichever is less.
    • Bank charges are yours to bear. Net banking is usually cheapest.

    14. Common mistakes to avoid

    1. Choosing Float but not uploading clear documents on time, which leads to seat cancellation.
    2. Not joining a Round 2 upgraded seat, which loses the deposit (and the Round 1 seat is already gone).
    3. Joining a Round 3 seat you don't really want. You cannot resign or move on.
    4. Listing colleges you wouldn't actually join.
    5. Ignoring Deemed University fees (often very high).
    6. Using a different phone or email from your NBE form.
    7. Paying through an agent or third party, or trusting "guaranteed seat" offers.
    8. Getting a PwBD certificate from a hospital that is not a designated board.
    9. Old OBC/EWS certificates (they must be for FY ending 31 March 2026).
    10. Closing the bank account or card used for payment before the refund arrives.

    15. Errors & unclear points in the draft

    Useful for counsellors. Watch whether these are fixed in the final bulletin.

    2. The NMC cover letter title says "2025" while the guidelines say 2026. It cites Section 25 of the NMC Act, while Chapter 5 cites Section 24.
    3. Chapter 5 refers to the "Graduate Medical Education Regulations, 2023" instead of the PG regulations. The RPwD Act appears once as "2015" (should be 2016).
    4. The PwD Assessment Board list is called Annexure-1 in some places and Annexure-2 in others.
    5. The EWS certificate format (Annexure-5) is blank, apparently a stray page from an older document.
    6. AMU internal eligibility mentions internships ending by 31 March 2026, then a 30 Sept 2026 cut-off.
    7. The DU internship cut-off (31 July 2026) differs from the general cut-off (30 Sept 2026).
    8. Annexure-6 says the new upgradation scheme still needs Supreme Court approval.
    9. Annexure-6 still refers to NEET 2025 for OCI eligibility.
    10. Round 2 text says documents are uploaded "at the allotted college", which clashes with the online-only Float process.
    11. The PwBD table for pre-clinical speech disability is unclear.
    12. The appellate reasons clause points to "Clause 12.4", which seems to mean Clause 11.4.
    13. Schedule: the academic session starts on 20 Nov 2026, before Round 2 reporting ends (23 Nov) and well before Rounds 3 and Stray.
